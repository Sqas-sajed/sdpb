\section{Purpose}

These notes explain how the rebuilt EFT pipeline is organized after re-reading
\emph{Crossing Symmetric Dispersion Relations in QFTs} and
\emph{Extremal Effective Field Theories}. The goal is to keep the program
aligned with the variables used in the papers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item the crossing-symmetric coefficients $W_{p,q}$ of Sinha--Zahed,
    \item the spin/$s_1$ dispersion kernels $B^{(\ell)}_{n,m}(s_1)$ and
          $D^{(n,m)}_{\ell,\alpha}$,
    \item the symmetric EFT coefficients $g_k$ used in Extremal EFT,
    \item the SDPB dual positivity problem written in terms of functions of
          mass and spin.
\end{itemize}

\section{Two polynomial bases}

\subsection{Sinha--Zahed basis}

After subtracting massless poles, Sinha--Zahed write the crossing-symmetric
amplitude as
\begin{equation}
    M_0(s_1,s_2)=\sum_{p,q\ge 0} W_{p,q}\,x^p y^q,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
    x=-(s_1 s_2+s_2 s_3+s_3 s_1),\qquad
    y=-s_1 s_2 s_3,
\end{equation}
and $s_1+s_2+s_3=\mu=4m^2$.

For a finite EFT cutoff, only finitely many pairs $(p,q)$ with
$2p+3q\le K$ are kept.

\subsection{Extremal EFT basis}

For the massless identical-scalar EFT, Extremal EFT uses
\begin{equation}
    M_{\mathrm{low}}(s,t)=
    -g^2\!\left(\frac1s+\frac1t+\frac1u\right)-\lambda
    +g_2 (s^2+t^2+u^2)+g_3 (stu)+g_4 (s^2+t^2+u^2)^2+\cdots .
\end{equation}

The ring of symmetric polynomials is freely generated by
\begin{equation}
    E_2=s^2+t^2+u^2,\qquad E_3=stu.
\end{equation}
Therefore the translation problem from the Sinha--Zahed basis to the
Extremal-EFT basis is an exact linear change of basis between
$x^p y^q$ and $E_2^r E_3^q$.

\section{Positivity bounds from dispersion relations}

\subsection{Inversion formula}

The Sinha--Zahed dispersion relation gives
\begin{equation}
    W_{n-m,m}=
    \int_{2\mu/3}^{\infty}\frac{ds_1}{s_1}\,
    \Phi(s_1)\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(2\ell+2\alpha)a_\ell(s_1)
    B_{n,m}^{(\ell)}(s_1),
\end{equation}
for $n\ge 1$, where $\alpha=(d-3)/2$ and $0\le a_\ell(s_1)\le 1$ by
partial-wave unitarity.

This is the point where the physical program should move away from the
ordinary $(s,t)$ monomial language and instead keep the kernels as
functions of:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item the spin $\ell$,
    \item the spectral variable $s_1$ (or equivalently the mass variable),
    \item the EFT truncation indices $(n,m)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Positive combinations}

For $m\le n$, suitable positive combinations of the kernels lead to
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{r=0}^{m}\chi_n^{(r,m)}(\mu,\delta_0)\,
    W^{(\delta_0)}_{n-r,r}\ge 0.
\end{equation}
These are the positivity inequalities used to constrain the EFT data.

\subsection{Null constraints}

For $m>n$, the corresponding coefficient in the crossing-symmetric expansion
must vanish. In the large-$\delta$ limit,
\begin{equation}
    B^{(\ell)}_{n,m}(\delta)=
    \frac{C_\ell^{(\alpha)}(1)}{\pi}\,
    \frac{D^{(n,m)}_{\ell,\alpha}}{\delta^{2n+m}}
    +O\!\left(\frac{\mu}{\delta^{2n+m+1}}\right),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    D^{(n,m)}_{\ell,\alpha}
    =
    \sum_{j=0}^{m}
    \frac{(-4)^j
    \left(-\frac{\ell}{2}\right)_j
    \left(\alpha+\frac{\ell}{2}\right)_j
    \left(\alpha+\frac12\right)_j
    (3j-m-2n)\Gamma(m-n)}
    {j!(m-j)!(j-n)!}.
\end{equation}

Hence the null constraints become
\begin{equation}
    \int_{\delta_0}^{\infty}
    \frac{ds_1}{s_1^{\alpha+1/2}}
    \sum_{\ell=2}^{\infty}
    (2\ell+2\alpha)a_\ell(s_1)
    \frac{C_\ell^{(\alpha)}(1)}{\pi}
    \frac{D^{(n,m)}_{\ell,\alpha}}{s_1^{2n+m}}=0,
    \qquad m>n\ge 1.
\end{equation}

The new module

implements the following data:

    \item JSON export of these kernels with the corresponding power

This is the correct object to store when one says that the null constraints
have been expanded into ``ordinary polynomials in spin and $s_1$''.

\subsection{Translation to the Extremal-EFT basis}

The older algebraic basis-conversion code is still useful, but now it is used
only for the coefficient translation problem:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item expand the Sinha--Zahed monomials $x^p y^q$,
    \item expand the Extremal-EFT monomials $E_2^r E_3^q$,
    \item solve the exact linear relation between the two finite bases,
    \item export the transformation matrix as JSON.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Two-sided bounds and SDPB}

Extremal EFT organizes the numerical bound problem in terms of functions
$g_k(m^2,J)$ and null functions $n_k(m^2,J)$ whose heavy averages obey
\begin{equation}
    g_k=\langle g_k(m^2,J)\rangle,\qquad
    0=\langle n_k(m^2,J)\rangle.
\end{equation}

The lower bound on $\tilde g_3$ is obtained by maximizing $A$ such that
\begin{equation}
    0\le
    \bigl(-A,1,c_4,c_5,\dots\bigr)\cdot
    \bm{v}(m^2,J)
\end{equation}
for all masses and even spins; the upper bound is obtained by minimizing $B$
with
\begin{equation}
    0\le
    \bigl(B,-1,c_4,c_5,\dots\bigr)\cdot
    \bm{v}(m^2,J).
\end{equation}

To interface with SDPB, the mass variable is rewritten as
\begin{equation}
    m^2 = M^2(1+x),\qquad x\ge 0,
\end{equation}
so the constraints become polynomial inequalities in $x$ for each spin.

\section{Reliability checks}

The rebuilt code includes explicit checks of at least five examples:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item the quoted closed form of $D^{(1,2)}_{\ell,\alpha}$ at $\ell=2$,
    \item the same at $\ell=4$,
    \item the same at $\ell=6$,
    \item polynomial reconstruction of $D^{(1,3)}_{\ell,1/2}$,
    \item polynomial reconstruction of $D^{(2,3)}_{\ell,1/2}$.
\end{enumerate}

These checks are chosen to verify the new spin/$s_1$ program directly,
instead of only checking the older algebraic $(s,t)$ basis manipulations.

\section{Interpretation}

The main conceptual correction is:
\begin{quote}
The physically meaningful null-constraint pipeline is not primarily a list of
relations among ordinary $(s,t)$ Taylor coefficients. It is a list of
spin-and-$s_1$ kernels whose weighted spectral averages vanish.
\end{quote}

The algebraic coefficient relations are still useful, but they should be
understood as a projection of the deeper dispersion-theoretic constraints.
